Group Presentation Project: Instructions and Guidelines

Project weight: 15% of final course grade
Presentation dates: December 1 and December 3
Format: Group presentation (5 students per group)

1. Overview and Group Formation

You will work with classmates to research and present a topic relevant to the material covered in our course. There will be 6 groups of 5 students, with 3 groups presenting each day.

Students should form their own groups. Each group should choose one coordinator to email me the names of all five members. If you do not have a group, or would prefer that I place you in one, email me by October 23 and I will assign you to a group. Everyone must join a group or request placement by that date.

2. Topic Selection and Approval

Choose an interesting topic connected to our course material. Email me for approval before preparing your presentation. The group coordinator should include:

  • Proposed topic title.
  • A brief description (2–3 sentences) of what the group plans to cover.
  • Names of all group members.

Topics must be approved to ensure relevance and avoid unnecessary overlap between groups.

3. Schedule and Deadlines

October 23 Form your group and send the member names, or request placement in a group.
November 6 Email your proposed topic for approval.
November 13 Finalize your topic and obtain approval.

November 24 Finalize slides and rehearse as a group.

November 20 and November 24 are recommended preparation milestones, not submission deadlines. The presentation order will be confirmed after groups are finalized.

4. Presentation Requirements

  • 15 minutes per group, followed by 2 minutes for questions and answers.
  • Every student must present a portion of the material. With five members, plan for approximately 3 minutes per student.
  • Explain the selected topic accurately and clearly, at a level appropriate for classmates.
  • Use readable, well-organized slides; diagrams and examples are encouraged when helpful.
  • Practice together to stay within the allotted time.

5. Suggested Presentation Structure

Introduction

Introduce the topic, explain why it matters, and preview what the audience will learn. Aim to capture your classmates’ interest.

Main Content

Present the key ideas in a logical sequence. Prioritize the most important information and use transitions to connect points. Ask yourselves: What are we explaining, and what should our classmates learn?

Conclusion

Summarize the key takeaways and explain what your group concluded. Do not simply repeat earlier slides word for word.

6. Evaluation and Participation

The presentation is worth 15% of your final course grade. Evaluation will consider:

  • Introduction and clarity of purpose.
  • Organization and transitions.
  • Accuracy, relevance, and appropriate depth of content.
  • Readability and quality of visual aids.
  • Clear, meaningful conclusion.
  • Delivery and engagement.
  • Knowledge demonstrated during questions and discussion.
  • Overall presentation quality.

Students will also evaluate other groups. Attendance, active participation, and constructive feedback matter and will contribute to the presentation grade.
