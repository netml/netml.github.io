Individual Research Project: Instructions and Guidelines

Due date: December 5 at midnight
Submission: Upload one PDF file to Brightspace.
Project type: Individual assignment

1. Project overview

Choose a topic that interests you and is relevant to material covered in this class. Research a specific issue or problem related to that topic, examine solutions discussed in academic research, and explain what the evidence shows about their effectiveness.

Your report should be 1,200–1,500 words, excluding the reference list. Write in your own words and support your discussion with academic sources.

Required academic sources:

  • Undergraduate students: At least 5 peer-reviewed journal articles or conference papers.
  • Graduate students: At least 10 peer-reviewed journal articles or conference papers.

You may use Google Scholar to find relevant papers. Check that each source is a peer-reviewed journal article or conference paper rather than assuming every Google Scholar result qualifies.

2. Required report structure

Use the following three main sections and headings in your report.

A. Introduction — at least 300 words

Introduce your chosen topic and explain why it matters. Clearly identify the issue or problem you will investigate. State the purpose of your report and briefly explain what the reader will learn.

Example: Rather than beginning with “Cybersecurity is important,” a stronger introduction might explain a particular cybersecurity challenge, who it affects, and why reviewing existing solutions is useful.

B. Background Report — at least 600 words

This is the main part of your report. Address all three questions:

  1. What is the issue? Describe the problem and its significance.
  2. What solutions have researchers proposed? Explain the precautions, approaches, or solutions discussed in the academic literature.
  3. How well did they work? Discuss reported results, including successes, limitations, or failures.

Connect the studies into a coherent discussion. Group papers that address similar problems or propose related solutions. Compare their findings and use transitions to show how the research fits together. Do not write one disconnected paragraph summarizing each paper.

Example of synthesis: “Several studies examined approach A and reported improvements under certain conditions. Other researchers tested approach B, which addressed a different limitation but introduced additional costs.” Support statements like these with citations to the papers you read.

When evaluating solutions, explain the evidence: for example, what improved, what remained difficult, and whether results differed across studies. Avoid simply saying a solution was “successful” without describing why.

C. Conclusion — at least 200 words

Summarize the main insights from your research and explain what the findings suggest about the issue and proposed solutions. Connect your conclusion to the purpose of the report. You may identify an important remaining limitation or open question.

Do not repeat earlier paragraphs word for word or introduce a completely new topic.

Word-count reminder: The section minimums add up to 1,100 words, but the entire report must still be 1,200–1,500 words. For example, a report with approximately 350 words in the introduction, 700 in the background report, and 250 in the conclusion meets both requirements.

3. Citations and references

Use APA 7th edition for in-text citations and the reference list. Cite ideas, findings, and claims drawn from research papers, even when you paraphrase them. Include a References section after your conclusion with full details for every work cited in the report.

The reference list does not count toward the 1,200–1,500-word limit. Make sure your report meets the minimum number of qualifying academic papers for your student level.

Total 100

5. Common mistakes to avoid

  • Choosing a topic that is too broad or unrelated to class material.
  • Listing one paper after another instead of comparing related findings.
  • Describing solutions without discussing their reported results.
  • Making claims without citing the supporting research.
  • Using nonacademic sources in place of the required peer-reviewed papers.
  • Falling below a section minimum or outside the overall word-count range.
